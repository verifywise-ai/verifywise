# Vendor Research Agent Performance Roadmap

> **Last Updated:** 2026-10-29

**Goal:** Make the vendor research web agent measurably better: findings a person accepts more often, runs that finish in about a minute, fewer wasted model calls, and coverage of the sources that matter. The ideas are numbered 1 to 39 as in the conversation of 2026-10-27; the order below is the one the product owner approved.

**Branch:** `feature/vendor-agents-and-risk-page`. Nothing here is committed by the agent that wrote it; the product owner commits when the work is reviewed.

**Constraints that hold for every item**

- The agent runs on the organization's own LLM key, on any model: no function calling, no native provider search, no third-party service that needs its own account, key or environment variable. Keyless public sources (a public JSON feed, a public API) are allowed when their terms allow it.
- Pages obey `robots.txt`; every request goes through `guardedFetch` (URL policy, address policy, size and time limits). The search feed is the one declared exception.
- No unit test touches the network or a real model. Everything that does is injected.
- Logs carry ids only. Never log page text, URLs, queries, model output or keys.
- Stored phrases the UI maps to translated text stay a closed set; every new user-facing string gets `en`, `de` and `fr`.
- `PROMPT_VERSION` changes whenever a prompt changes.

## Baseline (three real runs, dev database, `deepseek-flash`, `vr-2`)

| Run | Searches / pages read | Time | Sources | Findings |
|-----|----------------------|------|---------|----------|
| OpenAI | 8 / 6 | 146.5 s | 37 (11 on the vendor's site) | 8 (the cap), all Possible / Major / High Risk |
| NimbusAI Cloud (unknown vendor) | 8 / 0 | 57.0 s | 45, none about the vendor | 0 |
| NimbusAI Cloud again | 8 / 0 | 50.9 s | 45, none about the vendor | 0 |

What the runs showed: all 8 searches are spent every time and reads are barely used; 11 of 15 quotes come from snippets of about 200 characters; the same BBC story on `bbc.co.uk` and `bbc.com` counted as two hostnames and gave `high` confidence; two URLs returned identical text; `platform.openai.com/%E2%80%9D` got into the evidence; one news cluster became three findings in two categories; web results carry crawl dates, not publication dates; steps, queries, rejected candidates and token usage are not stored; the UI stops polling after 2 minutes.

## What the search feed does (probed 2026-10-27)

The probes run against the public RSS feed through the same URL the engine builds.

- **Web feed** (`/search`): for a name it knows (OpenAI, Okta), every query returns the same ten results, whatever topic words follow (`Okta data breach`, `Okta SOC 2 report trust` and `Okta subprocessors` are identical). `site:` and `inurl:` are not operators: `site:okta.com subprocessors` returned a café. For a name it does not know (NimbusAI Cloud) it returns unrelated pages. Result dates are the crawl date.
- **News feed** (`/news/search`): topic words count, results are dated, and a name with no news returns no items. Long queries lose recall (`Okta fine regulator GDPR` gave one item, `Okta security incident` gave six): a name and one or two topic words work best.
- `mkt=fr-FR` localizes the news feed (a French query returned French articles).

So the web feed is for finding a vendor's own pages, not for topics; news is the topic search; the vendor's own site is read directly.

That is the finding for a person to check that R7 asked for. **The download belongs to the process, not to a run:** the two files are loaded once and kept for 6 hours (`createMemo`; a failure is forgotten at once), with a signal of their own (45 s); a run waits for the list at most the 8,000 ms of the records, and a run that cannot wait has the lookup failed as unreachable with no rest, so the first run after a start may miss the list and the next one has it. **Ruling on the fetch:** `csvFetch.ts` is a plain guarded GET (text/csv or text/plain, read whole, 40 s, a file that was cut is refused since a list with its end missing is not the list), not a fetch that asks `robots.txt` as `feedFetch.ts` does: the host serves a government list whose documented way to get it is these files, its `robots.txt` is a 404, and a module of its own lets the records tests mock it so that none reaches the web. **The CSV is read by hand** in time proportional to its length (`parseCsv` reads once from the front: quotes, quotes written twice, line breaks inside a field, an unclosed quote ending at the end of the text; at most 400,000 rows), and a text that holds no entity is not the list. The hosts are in `Servers/.env.example` and `docs/deployment/CONFIGURATION_REFERENCE.md`, and `VENDOR_RESEARCH_RECORDS=false` turns the lookup off with the others. **(b) `financial_health` is a sixth category** (layoffs, a funding shortfall, insolvency or bankruptcy, or a sale or a change of control of the vendor, that threatens the continuity or the quality of its service; lookback 12 months), researched like the other news categories: three more queries for every vendor (`<name> layoffs`, `<name> bankruptcy`, `<name> acquisition`, and `<other name> layoffs` for a vendor with other names), in English for every market (no query in the language of a market, as for `availability`), a loop of its own, one more hint (`HINT_CATEGORIES`), and the same ledger, rules and verification. **The topic words:** R7 named funding, but a search for `funding` finds mostly good news (a round raised, a record year) that the model would have to throw away; `layoffs`, `bankruptcy` and `acquisition` find what threatens a customer, and the prompt says that a source about the finances is a risk only when it shows that the continuity or the quality of the service is threatened: a funding round, growth or hiring is not. A sale counts because the price, the terms or the availability of the service may change with the owner. **No database:** no free keyless source holds the finances of a private company, so the category is the news alone, and what the press does not cover is not seen. **The budget is split again:** a vendor with a domain has six loops, the two of the site one search each, the four news categories share the other six (2, 2, 1 and 1 in the order of the categories) and the 12 reads are 2 each; a vendor without a domain has four loops with 2 searches and 3 reads each. The routine work makes 10 news searches instead of 7 (at most 18 with other names and hints, 20 for a vendor with a market, all inside the 30,000 ms). **One migration** was enough, as R7 said: the one of R36 had already widened the CHECK of `vendor_risk_findings.category` for both categories. The client has the type, the label `Financial health` and its translations (German, French, Spanish). **`PROMPT_VERSION` is `vr-6`** for the whole of phase 5 (the convention of R29): the prompts list the sixth category and the sanctions list, and the words for the number of categories are made from the list of categories. Eval cases 20 `sanctions-list-name-match` and 21 `financial-health-layoffs-and-sale`; the cases that gather news record the three new searches. If wrong: a company with a common name that is on the list gives a finding to check (at most 2 records, low confidence, and the text says what to compare), and a vendor that is on the list under a name that the files do not give is not seen; the first run after a start may not have the list; an `acquisition` search finds many purchases that the vendor itself made, which are no risk (the prompt says so, and the model drops them); the layoffs of a large vendor are routine news, and a finding on them is a possible risk for a person to weigh; a private company that the press does not cover gets no finding either way; the three searches add a few seconds to the routine work. |
